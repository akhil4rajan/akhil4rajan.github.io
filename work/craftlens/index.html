Work / CraftLens

CraftLens In development

An on-device AI coach for hands-on crafts. Skill one: Haircut Coach.

New barbers learn a cut by watching a mentor, but the mentor can't watch every practice cut, and a section lifted at the wrong angle only shows once the hair is cut. CraftLens watches the trainee's hands through an iPhone on a tripod, measures the elevation angle live on the Mac, and talks them through the cut step by step. Everything runs on Apple devices with Apple Intelligence: no cloud calls and no API keys.

How it works

The first recipe is a uniform layer at 90° on a mannequin, in 6 steps, each held to ±10°. The trainee scans the mannequin with the CraftLens Scan iPhone app, sees the sections and guide lines on the 3D head, then cuts with the coach. Afterwards they get a review per step, a spoken debrief, and progress across cuts.

Three loops run side by side and never wait on each other: Vision measures the hand's angle about 15 times a second; the on-device image model looks every few seconds to see which small move the trainee is on (sectioning, combing out, pinching, lifting, cutting); and visual checks run when a step ends. Guidance fades from Beginner to Practising to Confident as the trainee improves.

  • The AI never sets the angle

    Vision measures, the recipe sets the target, the coaching engine picks every cue. The language model only explains, from the recipe.

  • The image model observes

    It can only answer looks right, concern or can't tell, and a concern is spoken in the recipe's own words.

  • The mentor curates the library

    Answers come from the salon's approved documents and shop rules, with the source shown.

  • Private by design

    Client faces never leave the salon's devices. The iPhone-to-Mac link is local and encrypted.

New skills

CraftLens is built so a new skill is a recipe file, not new app code. The coaching engine is plain Swift with no Apple-only frameworks, so the same engine can later drive an iPhone-only app.

  1. Write the recipe with a mentor: steps, target and tolerance, the small moves and what each looks like, help text, visual checks and coaching lines.
  2. Pick the measurement. Reuse the hand-angle detector, or add one detector for what the new craft measures.
  3. Load the library with the workshop's own manuals and rules, approved by the mentor.
  4. Validate, then ship. Check the measurement against the tolerance and score the visual checks on real stills before the coach speaks them.

Next come more haircut techniques on the same detector, such as graduation at a lower lift. After that, other crafts where angle and order matter, explored with experts: sharpening a blade, holding a soldering iron or torch, calligraphy pen angle.
