CraftLens 🏆 Hackathon winner

An on-device AI coach for hands-on crafts. Skill one: Haircut Coach.

Built in 44 hours at the AI On Apple Sydney Hackathon, where it won 1st prize in the Enterprise category.

“My students need real-time feedback when they practise, but I can’t be there to guide every cut.”Ash, barber with 18 years of experience

New barbers learn a cut by watching a mentor, and a section lifted at the wrong angle only shows once the hair is cut. CraftLens watches the trainee's hands through an iPhone on a tripod, measures the elevation angle live on the Mac, and talks them through the cut step by step. Everything runs on Apple devices with Apple Intelligence: no cloud, so clients' faces never leave the salon.

How it works

The first recipe is a uniform layer at 90° on a mannequin, in 6 steps, each held to ±10°. The trainee scans the mannequin with the CraftLens Scan iPhone app, sees the sections and guide lines on the 3D head, then cuts with the coach. Afterwards they get a review per step, a spoken debrief, and progress across cuts.

Three loops run side by side and never wait on each other: Vision measures the hand's angle about 15 times a second; the on-device image model looks every few seconds to see which small move the trainee is on (sectioning, combing out, pinching, lifting, cutting); and visual checks run when a step ends. Guidance fades from Beginner to Practising to Confident as the trainee improves.

  • The AI never sets the angle

    Vision measures, the recipe sets the target, the coaching engine picks every cue. The language model only explains, from the recipe.

  • The image model observes

    It can only answer looks right, concern or can't tell, and a concern is spoken in the recipe's own words.

  • The mentor curates the library

    Answers come from the salon's approved documents and shop rules, with the source shown.

  • Private, offline and free to run

    The LLM runs on the Mac, so every call is private, works without internet and costs nothing. The iPhone-to-Mac link is local and encrypted.

Beyond barbering

Barbering is just skill one. The same approach can teach any hands-on skill: watch what you do, compare it with how an expert does it, and coach you in the moment. Each new skill is a new recipe, not a new app.

  • Trades

    Tool angles, the order of steps and safe technique, checked while the work is happening.

  • Cooking

    Knife work and technique, talked through step by step.

  • Painting

    Brush angle, strokes and sequence compared with how the teacher does it.

  • Playing an instrument

    Hand position and posture while you play, with feedback in the moment.

How a skill gets added: an expert maps the steps, the targets and what good looks like into a recipe; CraftLens reuses the hand-angle measurement or adds one new measurement; the expert's own manuals go in the library; and it's checked against the expert before it coaches anyone.

Hackathon

CraftLens went from idea to working app in 44 hours at the AI On Apple Sydney Hackathon and won 1st prize in the Enterprise category, with a team that met on the Friday night: Akhil Rajan, Andy Rana, Alexandra Shevtsova and Yining Du. Everyone brought something different: code, design, the pitch and talking to real barbers.

What stood out: Apple Intelligence on-device is far more capable than expected. With the Foundation Models framework the LLM runs right on the Mac, guided generation gives structured answers the app can rely on, and the model can look at images. Combined with Vision, Speech and RealityKit, a small team can build a real multimodal product in a weekend, with no API keys.
