Work / CrewBrief

CrewBrief Latest release

Real-time voice AI for utility field crews. Built on Azure.

Before every job, a utility crew fills in a hazard assessment (JSA / SWMS) by hand, which takes around 40 minutes. The crew already talks through the hazards in the pre-job briefing. CrewBrief listens to that briefing, drafts the form from what was said, asks about anything critical that was missed, and hands it to the crew lead to check and sign. The form is never submitted without a person.

Demo

Demo video: coming with this release. The walkthrough below is the script it follows.

Three scripted briefings, one per scenario. The full demo script is on GitHub, with steps to run it yourself.

  1. Record. The crew lead starts a briefing and reads the recording notice. CrewBrief never interrupts: the form fills in on the side as people talk, and each voice shows up as a speaker.
  2. Clarify. At the end, CrewBrief checks for critical gaps and asks up to three questions out loud, e.g. "I heard work near live LV, but no isolation or earthing control. What's the plan?"
  3. Check. One card at a time for each item that needs a decision: a typical hazard nobody mentioned, a hazard without a control. Blocking items come first.
  4. Sign. The finished JSEA as a paper-style form: permits, hazards with risk before and after controls on a 5×5 matrix, PPE suggested from the job's hazards, emergency plan, job steps and crew sign-on. The crew lead signs and submits; the rest of the crew taps I've read the form.

The scenarios: an LV service connection where the isolation plan comes up late, a well-run pole replacement with a crane near live LV, and a night storm callout with a tired crew where the critical gaps stay open until Clarify.

Numbers

  • 40 → 5minutes per form: written by hand today vs. reviewed with CrewBrief (target)
  • 100%critical hazard recall (target ≥ 98%)
  • 100%of filled fields linked to what the crew said (target ≥ 95%)
  • 0%unsupported, made-up fields (target ≤ 2%)

Measured offline on 6 synthetic briefings; the same evaluation runs as a gate on every pull request. Next step is 20 real briefings scored by safety experts, then a shadow pilot.

How it works

Azure AI Speech transcribes the briefing and separates the voices. A pipeline of narrow AI agents does the rest: the analyst pulls out tasks, site conditions and hazards, each linked to the transcript lines behind it; the hazard expert grounds them in the company's hazard and control library; the form filler writes updates to the draft; the verifier looks for unsupported fields and critical hazards with no control.

The orchestrator is a deterministic state machine, not an LLM. It decides what runs when, which keeps the live path fast (about 2 seconds from speech to an on-screen field is the target) and makes every decision auditable.

  • Evidence for every field

    Each hazard and control links to the exact words the crew said.

  • Safety rules in code, not prompts

    A critical hazard needs an isolation or engineering control. A missing control is never assumed.

  • A person always signs

    Blocking items must be settled, or overridden with a written reason, before sign-off.

  • Private by default

    No raw audio stored, no voice biometrics, a recording notice every briefing, data kept in Australia.
